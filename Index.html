Introduction

The environment is everything, living and non-living, that surrounds us. It provides the air, water, food and materials we need. Its components fall into two groups, biotic and abiotic, and both work together to keep ecological balance and support life on Earth.

Biotic components

The living parts of the environment.

Plants
Producers of food, release oxygen, maintain ecological balance.
Animals
Maintain the food chain, help pollination and seed dispersal, keep the ecosystem balanced.
Humans
Use natural resources, are responsible for conservation, shape the environment.
Microorganisms
Decompose dead matter, help nutrient cycling, maintain soil fertility.

Abiotic components

The non-living parts that set the basic conditions for life.

Air
Provides oxygen, shapes weather and climate.
Water
Essential for all living beings, regulates temperature.
Soil
Provides nutrients to plants, supports life forms.
Sunlight
Source of energy, drives photosynthesis.
Temperature
Controls life processes, affects climate and weather.
Minerals
Needed for plant and animal growth, found in soil, water and rocks.

Food chain: how energy moves

☀️ Sun→🌱 Producer→🐛 Herbivore→🦊 Carnivore→🦠 Decomposer

Producers turn sunlight into food. Each level passes on part of that energy. Decomposers return nutrients to the soil, so the cycle starts again.

Natural cycles that link both groups

Water cycle

Sun heats water, which evaporates, forms clouds, and falls as rain. Plants absorb it and animals drink it, and it returns to the air and rivers.

Carbon and oxygen cycle

Plants take in carbon dioxide and release oxygen during photosynthesis. Animals, humans and microbes breathe in oxygen and release carbon dioxide.

Nutrient cycle

Minerals in soil feed plants. Animals eat plants. When living things die, microorganisms break them down and the minerals return to the soil.

What disturbs the balance?

  • Deforestation: fewer plants means less oxygen, less habitat and poorer soil.
  • Pollution: contaminated air, water and soil harms all living things.
  • Climate change: shifting temperature and rainfall affect where species can live.
  • Overuse of resources: water, minerals and fuels are used faster than they renew.
  • Loss of biodiversity: removing one species can disturb the whole food chain.

Changing one component affects the others. For example, polluted water harms fish, which harms the animals and humans that depend on them.

What we can do

  • Plant trees and protect green spaces.
  • Save water and electricity.
  • Reduce, reuse and recycle waste.
  • Use public transport, cycling or walking when possible.
  • Avoid single-use plastic.
  • Spread awareness among friends and family.

Quick check

1. Is soil biotic or abiotic?

Abiotic. Soil itself is non-living, but it supports many living organisms.

2. Which component is the source of energy for ecosystems?

Sunlight. It powers photosynthesis in plants.

3. What do microorganisms do?

They decompose dead matter, cycle nutrients and keep soil fertile.

4. Why do both groups matter?

They are interconnected. Living things need non-living conditions, and living things change those conditions in return.

Importance

  • Maintains ecological balance
  • Provides resources for life
  • Supports biodiversity
  • Helps in climate regulation
  • Ensures sustainable living

Conclusion

Both biotic and abiotic components are essential for life on Earth, are interconnected, and depend on each other. To keep a healthy environment we must protect all its components and use natural resources wisely.
